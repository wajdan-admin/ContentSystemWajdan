# PRINCIPLES

## 1. The modern story arc: stack peaks, do not build to one
**Source:** @kallawaymarketing, 418K plays, 13.7K likes, **17.1K comments**
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DY2BBsIuFcC/

The traditional arc taught in school is conflict, rising action, climax, falling action,
resolution. It fails on social because people scroll away before the rising action gets good.

The modern arc:

- **Do not start at zero. Start at 70 out of 100.** The first five seconds should feel like being
  dropped into a story already in motion.
- **Spike to 90 within five to ten seconds.** This is the first peak. A contrast moment, a
  contrarian statement, a shocking number. Something that releases dopamine early.
- **Then release for twenty to thirty seconds.**
- **Then peak again.** And release. And peak. Repeat until the video ends.
- There is no single climax in the middle. You stack mini peaks and re-hook as long as you can.

**What this means for our scripts.** Most of what we have written has a strong opening peak, a
flat middle, and one hard final line. By this model the flat middle is exactly where people leave.

Honest audit of what exists:

| Script | Peaks | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Five things | 5, one per number | Strong. Every "number three is" is a re-hook |
| Nobody buys your expertise | 3 fast categories, then mistake, then reframe | Good |
| The rule of 4, 20 and 60 | 3 numbers, then the turn | Good |
| I don't gatekeep | 6 screen cuts | Good, the cuts do the work |
| Why do they pay you more | hook, then one long build | **Weak middle.** One peak at 0:00 and one at the end |
| The agencies that disappear in 2027 | hook, then one argument | **Weak middle.** Same problem |

**Rule going forward:** every script needs a peak roughly every twenty to thirty seconds. A number,
a reversal, a quoted objection, a short sentence that contradicts the one before it. If a stretch
of script has none, it is where the drop happens.

## 2. Complete sentences, but short
**Source:** Wajahat, 2026-11-26, reviewing the five things draft

Short does not mean fragmented. "A dentist is." is three words and a full sentence.
"The one thing you're best at." is six words and is not.

Fragments make the viewer assemble the meaning. A short complete sentence hands it over.
Keep sentences under about twelve words, but keep the subject and the verb.

Per item in a list, use three beats: **name it, say what it is, give the instruction.**

## 3. The CTA works better as the last item than as an add on
**Source:** @aymen_berrahma, 688K plays, **9.4K comments** (1.37% of plays)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYFYlk2DygH/

His framework had four steps and step four was "comment YouTube". The ask read as an instruction
inside the sequence, not as a plea bolted on the end. Nine thousand people completed it.

Where a script is a numbered list, make the CTA the final number. Put the proof **after** the ask,
as the reward for doing it, not before it as a boast.

---

## 4. Comments beat likes as a target
**Source:** observed across @kallawaymarketing (17.1K comments vs 13.7K likes) and
@aymen_berrahma (9.4K comments on 688K plays)

Both ran a keyword CTA and both got comment counts that are abnormal for their view counts.
Kallaway got **more comments than likes**, which almost never happens.

A keyword CTA is the single highest leverage line in a reel, but only when there is something real
to send. Promising a document that does not exist burns the warmest viewers in the audience.

## 5. Production value is inversely correlated with performance
**Source:** the outlier dataset, November 2026

Across every comparison in `formats.md`, stick figures, cars, single takes and phone footage beat
studio work. Polished reads as an advertisement. The effort tags on the highest multiple posts
are almost all "within an hour".

## 6. Subject beats mechanism
**Source:** the outlier dataset, November 2026

Money, pricing, buyer psychology and identity outperformed system and mechanism explainers by
roughly ten times median multiple in this niche. Explaining how something works is the lowest
performing thing you can do with a reel.

---

## 7. Do not define a word inside a reel
**Source:** Wajahat, 2026-11-26

If a word needs explaining, it is the wrong word. "Vertical" was cut for this reason, and so was
"cover" as one of three things people buy. @danmartell does the right version of this: he never
defines "niche", he just says "small businesses is not a niche, a dentist is".

Show the wrong answer, then two or three right ones. Faster than a definition and it sticks.
